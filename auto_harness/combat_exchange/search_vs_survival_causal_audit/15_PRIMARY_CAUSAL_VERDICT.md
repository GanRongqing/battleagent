# Search vs Survival Causal Split

## Verdict
**SURVIVAL_GEOMETRY** (upstream); SEARCH is a shared, non-differential weakness.

## Earliest Divergence
The **first White combat-USV contact** occurs at ≈5,400–6,300 s for both WIN (W2-2 ≈6,270 s) and LOSS
(W2-6 ≈5,370 s); before that, visible tracks are **0 for both groups** (search/patrol produces no
differential contacts). The first divergence between WIN and LOSS is therefore **at/after contact**,
i.e. in the engagement, not in pre-casualty search.

## Strongest Evidence
1. **No pre-contact track differential.** Both WIN and LOSS hold vis≈0 until the enemy arrives
   (≈5–6k s); the 16.4-vs-7.35 "peak visible tracks" gap is a whole-episode max dominated by the
   combat phase, not the search phase.
2. **Contact timing is not worse for LOSS.** W2-6 first contact ≈5,370 s (vis=1) ≈ WIN W2-2 ≈6,270 s.
   W2-1 first death (27,270 s) even **precedes** its first contact (29,070 s) — the White USVs are
   lost without ever acquiring — but this is a late, catalogue-wide failure, not an early search gap.
3. **Outcome splits after contact.** At contact WIN survives (final alive ≈8–13) while LOSS is wiped
   (final alive ≈0–1); the surviving-sensor count then explains the later visible-track collapse.

## Evidence Against SEARCH (as primary differential)
Pre-casualty, alive sensing assets (20 USVs + 10 UAVs) are **equal** across groups (no deaths yet), and
visible tracks are **equal (≈0)**. A search-coverage differential would have to show *more* contacts for
WIN before any casualty — it does not.

## Evidence Against SURVIVAL
W2-1 loses USVs *before* acquiring (search genuinely failed there), so search is not irrelevant — it is
a shared weakness that amplifies the loss, not the differential driver.
